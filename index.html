How the count works

Square footage is not a board count. DeckBoardCalc counts rows, then buys whole stock boards that cover those rows. For a 16 ft by 12 ft deck with 5 1/2 in boards, a 1/8 in gap, and boards running the 16 ft way, that is 26 rows. At 10% waste you buy 29 boards of 16 ft.

  1. Pick the board run. Rows stack across the other side.
  2. Rows = (across + gap) ÷ (board width + gap), rounded up. There is no gap after the last board.
  3. If a stock length covers a row, use it. If one board cuts into two or more full rows, those pieces count. Among lengths that work, the list buys the fewest linear feet after waste.
  4. If every board is shorter than the row, the row is spliced from the mix with the least waste. The butt joint has to land on a joist when you build.
  5. Waste (default 10%) adds extra rows, then rounds up to whole boards. Offcuts are already in the cut plan. Waste is on top of that.
  6. Joists: one at each end of the run, plus every spacing you picked. Screws: 2 per board per joist, then the same waste percent.
  7. Cost is your price times what you buy. The defaults are a ballpark, not a quote.

Your deck, worked out

    Common questions

    How many deck boards do I need for a 12×16 deck?

    A 12×16 deck is not one number. It depends which way the boards run. With 5 1/2 in boards (the face of a 5/4×6 or a 2×6), a 1/8 in gap, 10% waste, and 8, 10, 12, and 16 ft boards available: boards along the 16 ft side, stacked across 12 ft, need 26 rows to cover the deck. Buy 29 boards at 16 ft, which is 464 linear feet. 416 linear feet actually sit on the deck. Boards along the 12 ft side, stacked across 16 ft, need 35 rows. Buy 39 boards at 12 ft, which is 468 linear feet. DeckBoardCalc opens on the first layout. That is the usual attached deck: joists run out from the house, and boards run along the house.

    How do you calculate the number of deck boards?

    Decide which way the boards run. The rows stack across the other dimension. Divide that distance by one board plus one gap, then round up. There is no gap after the last board, so the formula is (across + gap) ÷ (board width + gap), rounded up. Cover each row with stock you can buy. If one board is long enough to cut into two full rows, it counts twice. If nothing is long enough, splice the row from shorter boards. Then add your waste percent as extra rows and round up to whole boards.

    What waste factor should I use for decking?

    10% is a sound default for a simple rectangle with square cuts. Use about 15% if you are new to cutting, you expect to reject crowned boards, or the deck has angles, a picture frame, or breaker boards. Use 5% only when the stock length matches the run and you are comfortable with almost no extras. The percent sits on top of offcuts. A 16 ft board cut down to a 12 ft row already leaves 4 ft in the cut plan. Waste adds boards beyond that.

    How big a gap should I leave between deck boards?

    For pressure-treated wood or cedar, 1/8 in is the usual gap. A wet climate sometimes uses 3/16 in. Composite and PVC decking is different: use the gap the manufacturer prints, especially with hidden fasteners. A gap of 0 on wood can hold water and let boards buckle when they swell.

    Do board gaps change how many boards I buy?

    Yes. The gap is space you do not cover with wood. On a 12 ft width with 5 1/2 in boards, butting them tight takes 27 rows. A 1/8 in gap brings that to 26 rows, because the openings add up to more than the shortfall. DeckBoardCalc counts the gap. A square-foot shortcut that ignores it will not match the cart.

    What is the real width of a 5/4×6 or 2×6 deck board?

    A 5/4×6 is about 1 in thick and 5 1/2 in wide. The 6 is a nominal width. A 2×6 is about 1 1/2 in thick and also 5 1/2 in on the face. For a board count, both use 5.5 inches. A 2×4-style face is 3 1/2 in. Measure a board at the yard if the stock looks different from the label.

    How many screws do I need for a deck?

    Use two screws every time a board crosses a joist. On the opening example — 16 ft by 12 ft, boards along the house, joists at 16 in on center — that is 26 boards × 13 joists × 2 = 676 screws. Add 10% and you buy 744. That lands near the old rule of thumb, about 350 screws per 100 sq ft, for this layout. Hidden fastener clips are a different count. Follow the clip maker. The joist spacing control only feeds this estimate. It does not say whether 12, 16, or 24 in on center is allowed for your board or your span.

    How many joists do I need at 16 inches on center?

    Put a joist at each end, then fill the middle at the spacing you picked. For 16 ft at 16 in on center, 192 in ÷ 16 in = 12 spaces, and 12 spaces need 13 joists. If the length does not divide evenly, the last bay is shorter than the spacing, not longer. Those end joists are already in the count. You still need an outer rim board the length of the deck, and a ledger if the deck attaches to the house. This page does not check span tables, beam size, or footings.

    Should deck boards run the long way or the short way?

    Run them perpendicular to the joists. On a deck attached to a house, joists usually run out from the house, so the boards run along the house. That is the default here. The other direction is a different shopping list, not a smaller version of the same one. A 12×16 with boards on the 12 ft side wants 12 ft stock: 39 boards at the defaults above. Boards on the 16 ft side want 16 ft stock: 29 boards. Build the direction you are framing. A row that fits on one stock board avoids a butt joint.

    Why do I buy more linear feet than the deck surface?

    Two extras stack. Lumber comes in fixed lengths, so a 14 ft row cut from a 16 ft board leaves 2 ft on the floor. If one board can be cut into two full rows, the calculator uses that piece and does not double-buy. Separately, the waste percent (default 10%) adds whole extra rows for bad cuts and mistakes. The list shows linear feet on the deck and linear feet to buy.

    Is this deck calculator good enough for a permit?

    No. It is a shopping estimate for a rectangular deck: board count, a screw rule of thumb, and a joist count at the spacing you chose. It does not apply building-code span tables, footing sizes, ledger bolts, guards, or stairs. Check the plan against your local code. If you are unsure, ask a builder or the inspector before you buy or build.

    Can I change the board price?

    Yes. Switch between dollars per linear foot and dollars per board. The starting prices are a rough big-box ballpark for pressure-treated 5/4×6 decking (about $1.25 a linear foot), 2×8 joists (about $1.60 a linear foot), and screws (about $10 per 100). They are not a quote. Type the price at the yard and the total updates. Nothing is uploaded.
